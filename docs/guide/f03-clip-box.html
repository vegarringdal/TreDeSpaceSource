Clip Box

On this page

App layout · F3

The Clip Box layout

Press F3 (or pick Clip Box on the App Layout ribbon) to switch to the clipping-box workspace. The clipping box hides everything outside it, so you can study one area of a large plant without the rest in the way. The Clip Shape List on the right adds up to seven more shapes (spheres, cylinders and boxes) that keep an area visible or cut a hole into it. Nothing is deleted: switch clipping off and the whole model is back.

  1. Clipping Box ribbon — described group by group below.
  2. Hierarchy — the model tree. Click a row to select that item and everything under it; the box can then be fitted to it.
  3. 3D view — shows the clipped model, the box outline and its handles.
  4. Clip Shape List — extra clip shapes. See The Clip Shape List panel.
  5. Console — minimised. Click its tab to see messages, for example when clipping is switched on or off.

How the box and the shapes work together

  • Enable (Z) on the ribbon is the main switch for all of this: the box and every clip shape only clip while it is on. The clipping planes of F2 Clip Plane are separate and work whether it is on or not.
  • The box and normal shapes keep what is inside them. With more than one, you see what is inside any of them, so two boxes in different places show two areas.
  • Inverted shapes are holes: whatever is inside them is hidden, even inside the box or another shape. Flipping the box's Cut Dir turns the box itself into a hole.
  • If only holes are left (the main box hidden or flipped, no normal shape on), everything outside the holes is shown.
  • Clipping planes cut on top of all this.

Ribbon

The Clipping Box ribbon

The ribbon controls the main box. The extra shapes are set up in the Clip Shape List panel.

On/Off, Helper and Cut Dir

  1. Enable (Z) — switches clipping on or off, for the box and all clip shapes. When you switch it on after clicking a point on the model, the box starts as a 5 m cube around that point. If you have not clicked the model yet and the box has never been placed, it covers everything loaded. Otherwise it comes back where it was.
  2. Box (ALT 332) — shows the box's outline (orange) in the 3D view. The box's handles are only shown while the outline is on.
  3. Flip (ALT 333) — swaps the cut: hides what is inside the box and shows the rest. Press again to go back.

Gizmo Mode

Picks which handles the box shows in the 3D view, so you can shape it with the mouse.

  1. None (ALT 339) — no handles.
  2. Move (ALT 334) — three arrows. Drag one to slide the box along it.
  3. Rotate (ALT 335) — three rings. Drag one to turn the box. Hold Shift for 15° steps.
  4. Scale (ALT 336) — handles that resize the box.
  5. 6 Axis / 3 Axis (ALT 337) — only with Scale. 6 Axis gives a handle on every face: drag one face and the opposite face stays put. 3 Axis gives one handle per direction that grows or shrinks the box on both sides at once.

Two keys work on the handles from anywhere: M steps through Move → Rotate → Scale, and X hides the handles and brings them back as they were. Both act on a clip shape instead when one is armed.

Box Size: Fit Sel and its offset

  1. Fit Sel (F) — fits the box tightly around the selected items and switches clipping on. A turned box is put straight again. Greyed out when nothing is selected.
  2. Fit Sel +Off. (F F: press F twice) — the same, with the offset 5 added on every side, so you also see what is right around the selection.
  3. Focus On Set (ALT 341, on at the start) — when on, both Fit buttons also move the point the camera turns around to the middle of the box.
  4. Fit Scene (ALT 340) — makes the box cover everything loaded, with 0.5 m to spare on every side. It does not switch clipping on.
  5. The offset in metres (2.0 m at the start). Use − / + or type a value. The same offset is used by Unhide Sel+Off. and Unhide Each+Off. on the Selection Color ribbon.

Resize (Main) and Step

Change the main box one face at a time. The letters are the faces of the view cube: Left / Right, Front / Back, Bottom / Top. If you rename the cube's faces in Settings, the letters follow.

  1. L+ and the other + buttons — push that face out by one step; the box grows on that side only.
  2. L− and the other − buttons — pull that face in by one step. The box never gets smaller than 0.1 m.
  3. Step (ALT 342 / ALT 343) — how far one press moves a face or the whole box, from 0.1 m up (1.0 m at the start).

On a turned box, the faces turn with it: T+ always grows the face that was on top before you turned it.

Move (Main) and Additional

  1. L, R, F, B, Bo, T — move the whole box one step towards that face, without changing its size.
  2. Shapes (ALT 1212) — opens the Clip Shape List panel on the right, in case you closed it.

Panel

The Clip Shape List panel

The panel on the right holds up to seven extra clip shapes on top of the main box. Each shape is a sphere, a cylinder or a box, and either keeps what is inside it or, when inverted, cuts a hole. See how they work together. Shapes only clip while clipping is switched on with Enable (Z) on the ribbon. To open the panel on its own, press Shapes on the ribbon or ALT 675.

Common

  1. + Sphere (ALT 320) — adds a sphere. With something selected it is sized to enclose the selection; with nothing selected it is placed in the middle of the model.
  2. + Cylinder (ALT 321) — adds an upright cylinder, fitted the same way.
  3. + Box (ALT 325) — adds a box, fitted the same way. All three are greyed out once there are seven shapes.
  4. Save… (ALT 326) — downloads all shapes as clip_shapes.json.
  5. Load… (ALT 327) — loads a saved file. It replaces the shapes in the list.
  6. Delete all (ALT 328) — removes every shape.
  7. Hide all (ALT 322) — stops all shapes from clipping but keeps the list. Press again to bring them back.
  8. Hide main box (ALT 329) — takes the main box out of play; the shapes keep clipping. Use it when the shapes alone should decide what you see.
  9. Helpers (ALT 323) — shows or hides the outlines (purple) of all shapes.
  10. Gizmo — Move (ALT 311), Rotate (ALT 312) or Scale (ALT 313) handles for the armed shape. Greyed out until you arm a shape in its row. A sphere cannot be rotated.
  11. 6 Axis / 3 Axis (ALT 338) — only with Scale, not for a sphere. 6 Axis gives a box a handle on every face, and a cylinder one handle for its width plus one for each end. 3 Axis resizes evenly on both sides.

One shape

Each shape has its own block in the list, headed by its name and its kind (or off when it is switched off).

  1. Name — rename the shape. Left empty, the list shows Sphere, Cylinder or Box.
  2. On / off — switches this shape on or off.
  3. Invert — makes the shape a hole: it hides what is inside it instead of keeping it.
  4. Outline — shows this shape's outline (while Helpers is on).
  5. Gizmo — arms the handles in the 3D view on this shape. Only one shape can be armed; while one is, the main box shows no handles. Press again to disarm.
  6. Delete — removes the shape.
  7. Fit: Sel — fits the shape to the selected items (to the whole model when nothing is selected).
  8. +2m — the same, with 2 m to spare on every side.
  9. Center (button) — moves the shape to the middle of the selection (or the model) without changing its size.
  10. Center — X, Y and Z of the shape's middle, in metres. For a cylinder this is the middle of its bottom end.
  11. Radius of a sphere or cylinder, and the Height of a cylinder.
  12. Axis (cylinder only) — the direction the cylinder points: 0, 0, 1 is upright, 1, 0, 0 lies along X.

A box shape has a Size row instead of Radius. Its three values are measured from the centre to each face, so they are half the box's full width, depth and height.

Step by step

Common tasks

Look at one area on its own

  1. Select the area: click its row in the Hierarchy (here /HA-TELE-PA, the public-address system), or click it in the 3D view.
  2. Press Fit Sel +Off. (F F). Clipping switches on and the box fits around the selection, with the offset added on every side. Use Fit Sel (F) for a tight fit.
  3. Fine-tune the box: drag its handles (pick Scale under Gizmo Mode), or use the Resize (Main) buttons to push single faces in or out by one Step.
  4. Press Z to switch clipping off and on again to compare.

Walk through the plant slice by slice

  1. Make a thin box: fit it to the area, then pull the F and B faces in with F− and B− until it is a slice a few metres deep.
  2. Set the Step to the distance you want to move each time.
  3. Press B or F under Move (Main) to slide the whole slice backwards or forwards through the model.

Cut a hole to see behind something

  1. Select what is in the way (here the loudspeaker /SX-HA86-013A).
  2. In the Clip Shape List, press + Sphere. The sphere fits around the selection.
  3. In the sphere's row, press +2m to make it a little bigger, then press Invert. Everything inside the sphere is hidden, while the box still keeps the rest of the area.
  4. Make sure clipping is on (Enable, Z); shapes do nothing while it is off.

Show two separate areas at once

  1. Fit the main box to the first area with Fit Sel.
  2. Select the second area and press + Box in the Clip Shape List.
  3. Both areas stay visible and everything between them is hidden. To show only the shapes, press Hide main box.

Select everything inside the box

Select Inside and Select Intersecting on the Selection Color ribbon select the items inside the box or the enabled shapes, so you can colour, hide or export them.

Keep the clipping for later

Clipping is not kept when you reload the page. Save the shapes with Save… in the Clip Shape List, or save a viewpoint: it stores the camera with the box, the shapes and the clipping planes, and brings them all back when you open it. See F10 Viewpoint.
