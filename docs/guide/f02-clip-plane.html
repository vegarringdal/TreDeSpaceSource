App layout · F2

The Clip Plane layout

Press F2 (or pick Clip Plane on the App Layout ribbon) to switch to the clipping-plane workspace. A clipping plane is a flat cut through the whole model: everything on one side of it is hidden from view, so you can see into decks, rooms and equipment as if you had sawn through them. Nothing is deleted; switch the plane off and the model is whole again.

  1. Clipping Plane ribbon — described group by group below.
  2. Hierarchy — the model tree. Click a row to select that item and everything under it.
  3. 3D view — the cut shows here. A click on the model also marks the point where a plane starts when you switch it on.
  4. Console — minimised. Click its tab to see a line for each plane you switch on, flip or reset.

Three planes: X, Y and Z

  • There is one plane per model axis, each with its own row on the ribbon and its own colour: X (red) and Y (green) stand upright, facing along the X and Y axes; Z (blue) lies flat. The three rows of every ribbon group always belong to X, Y and Z, top to bottom.
  • You can use them one at a time or together. With two or more on, you only see what is on the kept side of every plane, which is handy for cutting out a corner.
  • A plane keeps the side its arrow points to: with the plane as it starts, that is the side with the higher coordinate (for the Z plane: everything above it). Flip keeps the other side.
  • The planes work on their own. They do not need the clipping box of F3 Clip Box to be on, and you can use both at once.
  • The X plane starts tilted 5° (its °el value is 5). For a straight vertical cut, set that value to 0 in Rotate mode.

Ribbon

The Clipping Plane ribbon

Each group has three rows: X, Y and Z. The pictures mark the controls of one row; the other rows work the same way for their own plane.

On/Off and Visibility

  1. The plane's name and colour — the same colour is used for its marker and handles in the 3D view.
  2. Enable (ALT 301 for X, ALT 304 for Y, ALT 307 for Z) — switches the plane on or off. When you switch it on, the plane starts at the point you last clicked on the model. If you have not clicked the model yet, it starts at the centre of everything loaded.
  3. Helper — shows a small 3 × 3 m square with diagonals on the plane, in the plane's colour, so you can see where it is. The cut itself is visible on the model either way.
  4. Gizmo — shows the plane's handles in the 3D view: arrows to drag it, or rings to turn it while the plane is in Rotate mode. Switch it off when the handles get in the way.

Helper and Gizmo only show for a plane that is switched on.

Position and Position Step

  1. Center — moves the plane to the point you last clicked on the model. Greyed out while the plane is off.
  2. Position — where the plane is, as a real coordinate in metres along its own direction (the X coordinate for the X plane, and so on), so you can type a value straight from a drawing. Use − / + to move it one step at a time.
  3. Position Step — how far one press of − / + moves the plane, from 0.1 m up.

Flip, Rotation and Reset

  1. Flip (ALT 302 / ALT 305 / ALT 308) — keeps the other side of the plane instead.
  2. Rotate (ALT 303 / ALT 306 / ALT 309) — Rotate mode. While it is on, the angle fields can be changed and the plane's gizmo shows rings to turn it instead of arrows to move it. Press it again to go back to moving.
  3. °el (elevation) — how steeply the plane's arrow points up or down, in degrees. 90 points straight up, 0 is level. − / + change it by 5°.
  4. °az (azimuth) — the compass direction of the arrow, in degrees. 0 points along X, 90 along Y. − / + change it by 5°.
  5. Reset All (ALT 310) — switches all three planes off and puts every value back to the start: position, step, angles, flip and Rotate mode.

The angles stay as you left them when you leave Rotate mode. A plane turned this way still has its Position value; − / + then move it along its new direction.

3D view

Moving a plane in the 3D view

With Gizmo on, an enabled plane shows handles where it is:

  • Arrows (normal mode) — three arrows that follow the plane. Drag the arrow that points out of the plane to slide the cut back and forth. The two arrows that lie in the plane move the plane's helper square sideways; the cut itself does not change.
  • Rings (Rotate mode) — drag a ring to turn the plane around it. Hold Shift while dragging to turn in 15° steps. The °el and °az fields follow.

The camera does not move while you drag a handle.

Step by step

Common tasks

Look at a deck from above

  1. Click on the floor of the deck in the 3D view.
  2. Press Enable on the Z row. The plane starts at floor level, at the point you clicked, and hides everything below it.
  3. Raise the cut to about head height: type a height 1.5 m higher into the Z Position, or set Position Step to 0.5 and press + three times.
  4. Press Flip on the Z row, so everything above the plane is hidden instead.
  5. Turn the view to look straight down (click the top face of the view cube). Use − / + next to the Z position to move the cut up or down a step at a time.

Cut a vertical section

  1. Click on the model where you want the cut.
  2. Press Enable on the X or Y row. The model is cut through the point you clicked.
  3. Drag the plane's arrow, or use − / + next to its Position, to move the cut. Set Position Step first if you need finer or bigger steps.
  4. To see the part that was cut away, press Flip.
  5. To start again somewhere else, click there and press Center.

Cut at an angle

  1. Switch the plane on as above, then press Rotate on its row.
  2. Drag one of the rings in the 3D view (hold Shift for 15° steps), or type the angles in °el and °az.
  3. Press Rotate again to get the arrows back and slide the angled plane.

Switch clipping off again

Press Enable on the plane's row to switch it off. Its angles, step and flip are kept for the next time, but when you switch it on again it starts at the point you last clicked. Reset All switches every plane off and forgets all their settings.

Keep a cut for later

The planes are not kept when you reload the page. To keep a cut, save a viewpoint: it stores the camera together with the clipping planes, the clipping box and the clip shapes, and brings them all back when you open it. See F10 Viewpoint.
