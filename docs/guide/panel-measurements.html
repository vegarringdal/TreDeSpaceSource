Panel

Measurement List

You take measurements with the tools on the Measurement ribbon (see F6 Measurements). Each finished measurement appears here as a row with its name and value. The list does not place points itself.

Open the panel

  • Press F6: the Measurements layout has the list on the right.
  • Or click List on the Measurement ribbon (ALT 710).
  • Or open the Panel ribbon tab and click Measurement List (ALT 674).

A tour of the panel

  1. Load & save — save all measurements to a file, load them back, hide them all or delete them all.
  2. Config — decimals, colour and the 3D point spheres. Closed by default.
  3. Measurements — the list. The number on the right is how many there are.
  4. The row header — the measurement's name and its value. Click it to fold the row up.
  5. The row — rename the measurement and switch its helpers on or off.

Values are in metres (m), the unit the model is stored in; areas in m², angles in degrees. A Point shows its X, Y and Z coordinates. Σ marks a path's total length, Ø a diameter, ∠ an angle and ⊥ a face-to-face gap.

Load & save

  1. Save… (ALT 721) — downloads every measurement as measurements.json, together with the decimals, the colour and whether they are muted. Greyed out while the list is empty.
  2. Load… (ALT 722) — opens a saved file. It replaces the measurements you have now. A message tells you how many were loaded.
  3. Mute all (ALT 720) — hides every measurement from the 3D view without deleting it. The button then reads Show all; press it to bring them back.
  4. Delete all (ALT 723) — deletes every measurement at once, without asking. This can't be undone.

Measurements are not kept when you close or reload the app. Save them to a file, or store them in a viewpoint, if you want to keep them.

Config

  1. Decimals — how many decimals values show, 0 to 6 (default 3). Angles show at most 2.
  2. Color — the colour of every measurement's lines and markers in the 3D view, and of the area fill. Pick a light colour on a dark model, or the other way round.
  3. Sphere — size (radius in metres) and colour of the 3D spheres that a row's sphere button puts on its points. Changing them also changes every sphere already shown.
  4. Sphere fill: solid — draws filled spheres instead of wire spheres. The value beside it sets how see-through they are; it is greyed out until solid is ticked.
  5. Spheres on — puts spheres on every point of every measurement. When all of them already have spheres, it reads Spheres off and removes them all.

Unlike the labels and lines, which are always drawn on top, the spheres sit in the model: parts in front of a point hide its sphere. That makes it easy to see which points are behind something.

One measurement row

  1. Name — type a new name. Enter starts a new line, and words between ** are shown in bold. The header shows the first line; the 3D view shows the whole name above the value. A new measurement is named after its tool: Line 1, Line 2 …
  2. Label — shows or hides the name and value in the 3D view. With it off, only the lines are drawn.
  3. Spheres — puts a 3D sphere on each point of this measurement (size and colour under Config).
  4. Perpendicular helper — for points placed with Shift or the Perpendicular lock: shows the dashed line from where you clicked to where the point went, with a right-angle mark. Greyed out when the measurement has no such points.
  5. Slope — Line only. Adds the slope to the label: the angle from horizontal and the fall in percent. For a vertical line only the angle is shown.
  6. Show / hide — hides this measurement from the 3D view; the eye is crossed out while it is hidden. It stays in the list.
  7. Delete — removes the measurement. This can't be undone.
  8. XYZ — Line and Path only: switches all three ΔX/ΔY/ΔZ legs on, or all off.
  9. X, Y, Z — draws one leg of the "staircase" from the first to the last point: how far it goes along that axis. The legs are drawn red (X), green (Y) and blue (Z).
  10. XT, YT, ZT — writes that leg's length on it.
  11. ΣXYZ — adds all three lengths to the main label (X: … / Y: … / Z: …).

An Angle row has one more button:

  1. Reflex angle — shows the angle on the other side (360° minus the angle), with the arc drawn on that side.

Step by step

Common tasks

See how far a line goes along each axis

  1. Measure the line with the Line tool (see Measure a distance).
  2. In its row, press XYZ. Three dashed legs, one per axis, now run from the first point to the second.
  3. Press XT, YT or ZT to write the length on a leg, or ΣXYZ to list all three in the main label. Press the slope button to add the slope as well.

Send measurements to a colleague

  1. Give the measurements clear names, so the file explains itself.
  2. Press Save…. Your browser downloads measurements.json.
  3. Your colleague opens the same model and presses Load…, then picks the file. Their current measurements are replaced by yours.

The file holds the measured points, not a link to the model. If the model has moved or changed since, the measurements stay where they were.

Clean up

  • Delete one measurement with its bin, or all of them with Delete all. Neither can be undone, so save first if you may need them again.
  • To take screenshots without the measurements, use Mute all instead: nothing is deleted.
  • The Measurement button under Quick Clear on the Home ribbon also deletes all measurements, after asking first.

Viewpoints

Measurement (viewpoint)

A viewpoint can carry its own measurements. They are edited in a second copy of this panel, Measurement (viewpoint) (ALT 689), which is part of the F10 Viewpoint layout. It works like the list on this page, for the viewpoint you have open. Its controls are locked until you press Edit on the bar at its top.

  • Only one set of measurements is shown at a time. While a viewpoint's measurements are shown, this panel says Muted and offers Unmute scene to bring your own measurements back.
  • Changes to a viewpoint's measurements are kept only when you save them to the viewpoint. See the F10 Viewpoint guide.
