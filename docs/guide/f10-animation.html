User guide · App layout F10

Animation

Show how something is put together, one step at a time: items move into place, appear and disappear, the camera follows and cards explain each step. You make objects from what you select and key them on the Timeline, much like Navisworks Animator. The model itself is never changed — leave animation mode and everything is back where it was. Numbered markers on each picture match the numbers in the text; click a picture to see it full size.

App layout · F10

The Animation layout

Press F10 (or pick Animation on the App Layout ribbon) to switch to the animation workspace. It opens the Animation ribbon, puts the Timeline (with the Overlay Preview behind it) under the 3D view, and the Key panel, the Overlay Editor and Set Color on the right.

  1. Animation ribbon — turn animating on and off, new / open / save, export, the animation panels, undo, and what the 3D view shows.
  2. Hierarchy — pick the items you want to animate here or in the 3D view.
  3. 3D view — shows the animation at the time slider, and the move / rotate gizmo while you pose items.
  4. Timeline — everything you need while animating: the toolbar (make objects, key, play, export) and a row for each object and for the camera, the clipping, the colours, the labels and measurements, the steps and the overlays. The Overlay Preview tab behind it shows the card you are designing.
  5. Key panel (with the Overlay Editor and Set Color behind it) — the values of the key you picked, or of the next key you are about to make.

How an animation is made

  • Objects are what moves: select one or more items and make them an object — a pump, a skid, a valve wheel. Each object has one row on the Timeline.
  • Keys are diamonds on an object's row: where the object is at that time. A key holds its place in the model plus a move and a rotate — never a change from the key before — so moving one key never shifts the others. Between two keys the object moves; before its first key it waits at that key, after its last key it stays.
  • A key can also hide, show or colour the object from its time on. Hidden stretches are hatched on the row.
  • The Camera, Clipping, Colour rules, Labels and Measurements rows key the view, the clip box and planes, Set Color rules, and the labels and measurements on screen the same way.
  • Steps are named stretches of time — "Base frame", "Pump", "Motor" — with a card that explains them. Playback can wait at the end of a step; the play buttons jump from step to step.
  • Overlays are shown for a while: a callout hanging from an object, or a title card, caption or info card anywhere on the screen.
  • A file can hold several scenes — "Assembly" and "Maintenance", for example — each with its own objects and rows.

Items only move and turn; they are never stretched or resized. Nothing is saved into the model: leave animation mode and every item is back exactly where it was. Animation mode also starts from a clean scene — colours, hidden items, items you moved with the Transform ribbon and your clipping are put aside, so an animation looks the same for everyone — and gives them back when you leave.

Ribbon

The Animation ribbon

  1. Animate (ALT 901) — starts animating. Press it again to stop (ALT 903 also leaves playback); every animated item goes back to where it was. Present next to it (ALT 1573) shows the 3D view alone with the play bar; its Close brings back the panels, ribbon and mode you had.
  2. New (ALT 960), Open (ALT 925) an animation file (*.tdanim.json) and Save (ALT 926) — downloads the animation as a file, including the look of its cards and callouts. It reads Save* when there are unsaved changes.
  3. Export — Video (ALT 1410), Step stills as a PDF (ALT 1494), and Brief for AI (ALT 1549): the brief Claude Code writes an animation from (see Build an animation with Claude Code, which also shows the shortcuts that make the item lists it needs).
  4. Panels — shows or hides the Timeline (ALT 692), Key (ALT 691), Set Color (CTRL+C), Overlay Editor (ALT 695) and Overlay Preview (ALT 696); a highlighted one is open. Drag one to place it where you want it.
  5. Motion paths (ALT 956) — draws the path each moving object takes in the 3D view, with a tick at each key. The picked key is a big yellow cross.
  6. Overlays (ALT 921) — shows or hides the step cards and callouts.
  7. Camera keys (ALT 1571) — on, the view follows the camera keys; off, you orbit freely while it plays or you scrub, and it stays off through Play, Continue and starting again until you switch it on, press R or key the camera. Moving the view yourself only frees it until the next Play or Continue. Greyed out while the scene has no camera keys.
  8. Undo / Redo (CTRL+Z / CTRL+Y while animating) — undo and redo animation changes; they have their own history.

When the viewer is embedded in another web page, that page can add a Bookmark section at the end of the ribbon. Its button (ALT 1572) sends the animation as it is to the page — to keep it with your project, for example — and the page can open and present it again later.

Your work is also kept automatically in the browser. If the app is closed before you save, the Timeline offers the unsaved animation at its top the next time: Recover (ALT 981) opens it, Discard (ALT 982) throws it away. Until you choose it is kept as it was.

Panels

The Timeline

Everything you do while animating is on the Timeline. Its toolbar wraps onto a second line when the panel is narrow.

  1. Scene ▾ (ALT 1588 opens it) — the scene you work on, by its name; switch scenes (ALT 924 goes to the next), rename it (ALT 1581), add one (ALT 1349 — both ask for a name) or delete it (ALT 1350).
  2. + Object (ALT 961) — makes the selection an object, named after the selected item, with a key at the time slider holding where it stands. Its ▾ makes many at once: one per child of each selected node (ALT 1582) — a group becomes its parts, a row each — or one per selected node (ALT 1583); Select all the objects (ALT 1584) selects every object's items. See Animate a group part by part.
  3. Move and Rotate (ALT 1510 / ALT 1511) — put the move or rotate gizmo on the current object (its items are selected if nothing is). Pivot ▾ next to them (ALT 1590 opens it) chooses what the object turns about when a key has no pivot of its own: the centre or a side of its box — Centre (ALT 1380), Bottom (ALT 1522), Top, Front, Back, Left, Right (ALT 1594–ALT 1598); ALT 1523 steps through them — or the gizmo's pivot as a point of the object (ALT 1352). The object stays where every key has it.
  4. Key (I, or ALT 943) — keys the current row at the time slider: the object's pose, the view, the clipping, the colour rules, the labels or measurements, or a new step.
  5. Delete key (ALT 944, or Delete on the Timeline) — deletes the picked keys.
  6. Previous / next key (ALT 945 / ALT 946) — move the time slider to the key before or after, on the current row (on every row when none is current).
  7. Auto-key (the record dot, ALT 947) — while on, every drag of the gizmo is keyed at once (a red frame round the 3D view shows it). Off, a drag only poses the object until you press Key.
  8. Look tools for the current object, from the time slider on — the eye shows or hides it (ALT 1553, whichever it is not at the time slider); the key colour; Colour (ALT 1326) keys it in that colour, and its ▾ has Own colour (ALT 1327), Transparent (ALT 1554), Opaque (ALT 1555), Hide and Show.
  9. Rules (ALT 1323) — keys the colour rules built in the Key panel on the Colour rules row. Its ▾ keys quick rules for the selection — any items, not only objects: colour it in the key colour (ALT 1556), make it transparent (ALT 1557), hide it (ALT 1558) or show only it (ALT 1559) — and for the whole model: hide everything (ALT 1560) or back to its own colours (ALT 1561). Edit the rules in force (ALT 1562) picks that key so the Key panel edits it.
  10. Overlay ▾ (ALT 1585 opens it) — puts an overlay on the Overlays row from the time slider for 3 seconds: a callout hanging from the selection (ALT 942), a title card (ALT 1346), a caption (ALT 1345), an info card (ALT 1347), a step card (ALT 1348), a warning banner (ALT 1663), or a design of your own.
  11. Camera (ALT 948) — keys the view at the time slider; its ▾ keys a saved viewpoint instead, adds an Orbit (ALT 1406) or hands the view back to the camera keys (R). Clipping (ALT 1341) keys the clipping.
  12. Time and the transport — type the time slider's time; to the start (ALT 1505, Home), previous step (SHIFT+J), Play / Pause / Continue (K or a short tap on SPACE; Continue alone is ALT 912), next step (SHIFT+L), Stop (ALT 904) and to the end (ALT 1506, End). Without a button: play faster / slower (ALT 910 / ALT 911, 0.25× to 4×, remembered), replay the step (ALT 905) and play the in / out range (ALT 1306). With the mode off, K here starts animating first.
  13. Loop (ALT 909) — start again at the end. Ping-pong (ALT 1512) — at the end, play back to the start.
  14. Snap (ALT 1308) — dragged keys and bars snap to other keys, step edges and half seconds. Zoom out / in (ALT 1310 / ALT 1309, or CTRL + wheel).
  15. Present (the screen icon, ALT 1573) — the 3D view alone with the play bar; Close on the play bar comes back to this layout. Export ▾ (the download icon at the right end, ALT 1587 opens it) — Video (.mp4), Step stills (PDF), and the brief for AI (see Build an animation with Claude Code).
  1. Steps — one bar per step, with its name. A small flag at the end of a step means playback waits there.
  2. Camera — camera keys. With no keys the view is yours while it plays; one key fixes the view; more keys make it glide (or cut) from view to view.
  3. Colour rules — Set Color rules from a time on. The Clipping row above it does the same for the clipping, and the Labels and Measurements rows below it for labels and measurements (see Show labels and measurements).
  4. Overlays — a lane for each overlay, named by its design and title, with its bar and its position keys.
  5. Objects — the object rows sit under it, with how many there are; the arrow folds them away. Click it for the object tools in the Key panel.
  6. An object's row — its keys (a hollow diamond holds the object still until it, then it jumps), a coloured bar where it moves between keys.
  7. A hatched stretch — the object is hidden there.

The Scene row at the top shows the scene by its name; click it for the scene's settings in the Key panel (name, end, loop, step card), right-click it to rename the scene or pin its end. Click a row's name to make it the current row — the row Key keys; for an object, its items are selected so the gizmo sits on them. Selecting an object's items in the 3D view makes it current too. CTRL-click an object's name to add it to the objects selected (or drop it again), and SHIFT-click to pick every object from the current one to it: their names light up, one gizmo drag poses them all and Key keys each. Click a diamond to pick the key: the time slider jumps to it and the Key panel shows its values; SHIFT-click or drag a box over empty space to pick several. Drag diamonds to retime them, drag a step's or overlay's bar or its edges. CTRL+C / CTRL+V copy the picked keys and paste them at the time slider (into the current row), Delete deletes them, ← / → step the time slider (SHIFT for a second) and the Timeline scrolls to keep it in view. Click or drag the ruler to move the time slider; SHIFT-drag it to mark an in / out range for a video. Drag the time slider — or a diamond, a bar or its edge — to the left or right edge and hold it there: the Timeline scrolls along, faster the further past the edge you go, and what you drag rides on the edge (so you can put a key past the end of the scene). Right-click a key or a bar for what you can do with it, a row's name for that row's actions at the time slider, or empty space in a lane — the time slider moves to the click first. The dashed line is where the scene ends: at its last key, step or callout, unless you pin it in the scene settings.

When the animation only plays back — presented by a web page that embeds the viewer, say — the Timeline says Playing back only and hides its editing tools; press Animate there to edit.

The Key panel

The Key panel shows what you picked. For an object row it shows the next key — the pose at the time slider that Key will key:

  1. Key — keys the pose (the same as I).
  2. Time, Move, Rotate, Pivot and Offset — where the object will be, and what Move and Rotate are about. Type values instead of dragging the gizmo: the object follows. Changing Time moves the time slider and takes the pose along.
  3. Arrives — how the object gets to this key: Smooth (eases in and out), Linear (an even speed), or Hold (it stays where it was and jumps at the key) — ALT 1516–ALT 1518.
  4. Discard (ESC, ALT 1524) — puts the object back where its keys have it.
  5. From the time slider on — Hide (ALT 1507), Show (ALT 1508), Colour (ALT 1326, in the colour below) or Own colour (ALT 1327): a key at the time slider that does just that.
  6. Object — its name; Pivot (as Pivot ▾ on the Timeline) and Path (Smooth curves it through its keys); Muted stops animating it; Delete object.
  7. Items — what the object holds: nodes added (+) and nodes left out (−), each with × to drop it, and how many items that makes. Add selection (ALT 1563) and Remove selection (ALT 1564) change it; Select (ALT 962) selects its items; Update from selection (ALT 1353) gives it the selected items instead and keeps every key. When a group says the same more simply, a Closest group line offers it (ALT 1565) — see A group minus a few.
  8. Slide in — from which side, how far (in metres) and how long: see Slide something into place.

Click a key on the Timeline, and the panel shows that key:

  1. Time — retimes the key; the time slider moves with it, so Key still re-poses it.
  2. Move and Rotate — where the object is at this key, measured from its place in the model.
  3. Arrives — Smooth, Linear or Hold, as above (ALT 1516–ALT 1518).
  4. Spin into this key (ALT 1385) — the object turns whole turns about an axis on the way here (a wheel, a fan): set the axis and the degrees (360 = one turn; ALT 1386 / ALT 1387). A key added in the middle of a spin takes its share of the turns.
  5. Visible, Colour, Opacity — what the key changes from its time on, or As before: it leaves it as the keys before it have it. Shown / hidden ALT 1382, visibility as before ALT 1525; colour as before ALT 1526; opacity on / off ALT 1527, − / + ALT 1383 / ALT 1384.
  6. Delete the key (or the picked keys).
  7. Pivot and Offset — what Move and Rotate are about, and what the object turns about on the way here: as the object, or the centre or a side of its box (Bottom, Top, Front, Back, Left, Right), moved by the Offset in metres. A side is the object's own side wherever its keys have put it: turned over, its Bottom turns with it. The rotate gizmo turns about this pivot; move the pivot with the Transform ribbon's Set pivot and the Offset follows (see Swing something on a hinge). Picking a side starts its Offset at zero, and changing them leaves the object where it is. A new key takes the pivot of the key the object is heading to — the next key, or after the last key the last one, so as you go on the pivot stays on the same spot of the object. A pivot that is a point (from a file, or a key made before this way of working) shows as Centre plus the offset to that point. ALT 1574 steps through the sides; ALT 1570 goes back to the object's own pivot.

Pick a step on the Steps row:

  1. Name and Text — what its card shows (**bold** and line breaks in the text).
  2. Start and End — steps never overlap; their edges stop at the neighbours.
  3. Pause at the end (ALT 1514) — playback waits here until you press Continue; a video waits too.
  4. Card — the scene's step card (ALT 1539), a card of its own (ALT 1540 — a design, its values, where on the screen, the text size ALT 1542 / ALT 1543, a colour theme) or none (ALT 1541). Notes below are for you, not the audience. Play this step (ALT 906) plays it once; Delete (ALT 1515) removes it.

Pick the Steps row itself and the panel offers Add a step here and the scene's step card; the Overlays row offers Add an overlay ▾ (ALT 1586 opens it); the Objects row the object tools of Animate a group part by part. ALT 1372 takes the panel back to the scene.

Pick a camera key and the panel shows where it looks from — Position — and what it looks at — Target — in model coordinates (metres); type new values and the view follows. It arrives with a Glide (ALT 1408), at an Even speed (ALT 1528) or as a Cut (ALT 1409). Take this view (ALT 1529) gives it the view you have now, Go there (ALT 1530) looks through it.

A clipping key works the same way: Glide, Even or Switch (ALT 1343, ALT 1531, ALT 1344), Take this clipping (ALT 1532) gives it the clipping you have now, and Go there (ALT 1533) shows it.

The Colour rules row holds the Set Color rule editor itself. Pick a rules key and you edit it in place — the scene shows each change at once, and one burst of edits undoes in one step. With the row current and no key picked it is the next rules key: add rules (they show on the scene, on top of the rules in force) and press Key rules. The key's mode is Reset, Append or Hide (ALT 1534–ALT 1536); + Rule (ALT 1566) adds one. Rules in force (ALT 1567) starts from what is shown at the time slider; From Set Color (ALT 1537) takes the Set Color panel's rules, whose Save and Load stay there — ALT 1324 sends these rules there, and Rules ▾ → Open Set Color (ALT 1538) opens it.

With nothing picked, the panel shows the scene:

Its name; where it ends — Pin here (ALT 1519) ends it at the time slider, Unpin (ALT 1520) lets it follow its content again; Loop and Ping-pong; the Step card every step shows unless it has its own (ALT 978 switches step cards on and off) and its theme; and Keep labels on screen (ALT 1340): labels hanging from objects never leave the view — when the camera moves so a label would be cut off, it jumps to the other side or is pushed back inside. Problems at the bottom of the panel lists anything that will not play as you expect — items not found in the loaded models, an item in two objects, an object moving while it is hidden — with Go to (ALT 988).

Pick an overlay (its bar on the Overlays row) and the panel shows it: its Design and Theme — Edit style… (ALT 976) opens the design in the Overlay Editor, Detach (ALT 977) gives this overlay a copy of its own — its values, and where it sits: the Anchor is the Screen, an Object it hangs from or a Point in the model (ALT 1650–ALT 1652). On the screen a Pin puts it in a corner or on an edge and X / Y fine-tune it (ALT 1653–ALT 1656); hanging from something, dx / dy set the label's offset (ALT 1657–ALT 1660) and Leader line (ALT 1661) draws the line to it, in a colour of its own. Clear place keys (ALT 1339) drops its position keys; Scale (ALT 1389 / ALT 1390) and Text size (ALT 1395 / ALT 1396) size it; Show / Hide times and the Enter / Exit effects say when and how it comes and goes; the bin (ALT 1388) deletes it. An overlay whose object was deleted is hidden and listed under Problems.

Overlay Editor and Overlay Preview

Step cards and callouts are built from a few blocks — boxes, text, images, icons, a progress bar. The Overlay Editor is where you change their look, and the Overlay Preview shows the result live while you work. Opening a design for editing brings both to the front.

  1. Library — your designs and themes. New ▾ (ALT 1591 opens it) starts one from a preset — ALT 1311 makes a step card straight away — and the bin (ALT 1599) removes one of yours; a theme holds the shared colours. A card without a theme is Auto: it follows the app — Light in the light theme, Dark in the dark one; pick Dark or Light (for a step card, the scene's step card or a callout in the Key panel) to force one. The preview's theme picker shows a design in each. Export ▾ (ALT 1593 opens it) saves the open design to a .tdoverlay.json file (ALT 1499), or all your own designs and themes in one file (ALT 1500); Import… (ALT 1501) adds a file's designs and themes to your library — if one of them has the id of one of yours but is made differently, you are asked whose to keep.
  2. Outline — the blocks of the design. Select one to edit it, add blocks with Block ▾ (ALT 1592 opens it; a text block ALT 1312, a box, image, icon, divider or progress bar ALT 1600–ALT 1604), move them up and down or remove them (ALT 1313–ALT 1315). Undo / Redo (ALT 1317 / ALT 1318) and Save (ALT 1319) are in the editor's toolbar. Under the outline, Block holds the selected block's colours, fonts, borders, spacing and text. Text can contain placeholders like {{step.name}}, {{step.text}} or {{step.index}} that fill in by themselves while the animation plays; Parameters add your own, such as a title each callout sets for itself.
  3. Preview (ALT 696) — shows or hides the Overlay Preview panel.
  4. Overlay Preview — the design, live. Fit zooms it to fill the preview; 720p, 1080p and 1440p show its real size on a screen that tall (ALT 1642–ALT 1645). Pick a background — dark, light or chequered (ALT 1646–ALT 1648) — and press Effect (ALT 1316) to replay how it appears. Sample values and Sample step let you type real text to see how the card copes; they are not saved.

Card size, at the top of the editor, sets how wide the whole card is: Auto (as wide as its text, between Min and Max) or Fixed (ALT 1611 / ALT 1612; the widths step with ALT 1605–ALT 1610). Text size (ALT 1502 / ALT 1503) makes every text of the design bigger or smaller at once. Every stepper and choice of a block has a hotkey too (ALT 1613–ALT 1641, Rich text ALT 1649) — hover it to see which. For font sizes and colours per card, add a number or colour value under Parameters and pick it in a text block's Size or in a colour field: every card using the design then sets its own. Press Save to store the design in your library; every card using it changes with it.

The play bar

When animation mode is on and the Timeline is not open — after Present, or in a layout without the Timeline (ALT+F2, full screen) while animating — a play bar floats over the 3D view — with several scenes it starts with the scene that plays, a menu to pick another (ALT 1589 opens it): 1 the step you are in and its name, 2 previous step, play / pause / continue and next step, 3 a slider to scrub, 4 Camera keys (ALT 1571, when the scene has camera keys): on, the view follows them; off, you orbit freely until you switch it on again — and 5 Close (ALT 903): after Present it brings back the panels, ribbon and mode you had, otherwise it leaves animation mode. On a tablet its buttons are bigger.

Step by step

Common tasks

Make an object and key it

  1. Press Animate if you are not animating already.
  2. Select the items that move together — in the Hierarchy (a whole node is easiest) or the 3D view — and press + Object (ALT 961). It gets a row on the Timeline and a key at the time slider holding where it stands.
  3. Move the time slider to when it should be somewhere else (drag the ruler, type the time, or ← / →).
  4. Press Move or Rotate on the Timeline and drag the gizmo — as often as you like; it only poses the object — or type the values in the Key panel's Next key.
  5. Press Key (I). Repeat for every pose: it moves from key to key.

Until you press Key, a banner over the 3D view says the object is moved — not keyed. Moving the time slider or playing drops that pose, and the object goes back to where its keys have it — press Key first. You can skip + Object: posing or keying a selection that is not an object yet makes it one. With Auto-key on, every drag is keyed straight away.

Change a key

  1. Click its diamond. The time slider jumps there, the object's items are selected, and the Key panel shows the key.
  2. Type new values, or drag the gizmo and press Key — it replaces the key at that time and keeps what it hides, shows or colours.
  3. Drag diamonds sideways to change when things happen; one drag is one undo step. Pick several (drag a box, or SHIFT-click) to move them together.
  4. To reuse a movement, pick its keys, CTRL+C (ALT 1402), put the time slider and the current row where it should go, CTRL+V (ALT 1403).

Hide, show or colour an object

  1. Make the object's row current (click its name) and move the time slider to when it should change.
  2. Press the eye or Colour on the Timeline (its ▾ for own colour, transparent or opaque), or Hide, Show, Colour or Own colour in the Key panel (or right-click the row). A key appears at the time slider; from there on the object is hidden, shown or coloured until a later key says otherwise.

A key both places the object and changes what it looks like. If you add a hide key in the middle of a movement, the movement passes through that key — usually what you want; move the key if not.

A group minus a few

An object can hold a whole group and leave a few nodes in it out — a module lifted into place without its foundation, a skid without the pipe that stays connected. A group follows the model: what is added under it later moves with it.

  1. Select the group in the Hierarchy and press + Object.
  2. Select the nodes that stay behind and press Remove selection in the Key panel's Items (ALT 1564). They are listed with a −: the group minus these.
  3. Changed your mind? Select one and press Add selection (ALT 1563), or press × next to it.

If you built the object from many nodes one by one, the Key panel may find the closest group — the group holding them all, minus the few you did not pick. Press Use it and the object is the group minus those, with exactly the same items.

Animate a group part by part

A rack of loudspeakers, a stack of modules, the panels of a skid: when a group is made of parts that each move on their own, make all the objects in one go.

  1. Select the group in the Hierarchy and choose + Object ▾ → One per child (ALT 1582). Each of its parts becomes an object, in the order the Hierarchy lists them, with a key at the time slider holding where it stands. Selected several nodes? One per selected node (ALT 1583) makes an object of each.
  2. The new rows sit under Objects on the Timeline. Click one to key it alone, as usual.
  3. To move several together, CTRL-click their names (or SHIFT-click for a run of them), drag the gizmo and press Key: each gets its own key. Select all the objects (ALT 1584) takes them all.

Nodes that are an object already are skipped, so pressing it twice does no harm. More than 50 objects at once asks first. The Objects row's Key panel has the same buttons.

Swing something on a hinge

  1. Make the object current and key it closed at the time it starts to open.
  2. Move the time slider to when it is open. In the Key panel set Pivot to the side the hinge is on (say Right): the rotate gizmo moves there. If the hinge is not in the middle of that side, type an Offset, or drag the pivot onto the hinge with the Transform ribbon's Set pivot (then Lock) — the Offset follows.
  3. Type the Rotate angle (or turn it with the gizmo) and press Key. It turns about the hinge, and on the way to the key it swings instead of sliding round.

Each key has its own pivot: a lid can lift about its hinge and then slide away about its centre. A key you add after the last one takes its pivot, so the hinge stays on the hinge as you go on.

Slide something into place

  1. Make the object current and move the time slider to when it should appear.
  2. In the Key panel open Slide in: pick the side it comes from (above, below, ±X, ±Y), how far away it starts in metres, and how long it takes.
  3. Press Slide in from the time slider (ALT 1509). It waits hidden away from its place, appears at the time slider and slides into place.

Move the camera with the story

  1. Click the Camera row. Set up the view and press Key (or Key view, ALT 948).
  2. Move the time slider, set up the next view and press Key again. The camera glides between the keys; pick a key and set Arrives to Cut (ALT 1409) to jump instead.
  3. For a turntable, frame the item and press Orbit (ALT 1406) on the Camera row: one turn around what you look at, in 8 seconds.
  4. To use a saved viewpoint, open the Camera ▾ menu on the Timeline and pick it: its view becomes a camera key at the time slider.

The view follows the camera keys while the animation plays and while you scrub. As soon as you move the view yourself it stays where you put it, so you can set up the next view and press Key; keying the camera, R, and Play or Continue hand it back to the animation — unless you switched Camera keys off, which keeps it yours until you switch it on. A scene without camera keys never touches your view.

Cut the model open during the animation

  1. Click the Clipping row and set up the clipping with the usual ribbons — clip box, clip planes or clip shapes.
  2. Press Key (or ALT 1341). Move the time slider, change the clipping, Key again: the box and planes glide from key to key — a section sweeping through the model. Set a key's Arrives to Switch to jump instead.

Hide or colour the plant during the animation

  1. Click the Colour rules row and move the time slider to when the rules should apply.
  2. In the Key panel add a Rule and set it up as in Set Color — for example Hide mode with a rule that keeps the foundation, or a rule that paints one system red. The scene shows it while you work (not keyed yet).
  3. Press Key rules (I). From that time on, the scene is coloured that way.
  4. Add another rules key later to change the picture again — Append puts its rules on top of the ones before, Reset starts from a clean scene. To change a rules key, click its diamond and edit it right there in the Key panel.

For the everyday cases you do not need to build rules at all: select the items, and the Rules ▾ menu on the Timeline keys a rule that colours them, makes them transparent, hides them or shows only them from the time slider on. Hide everything and The model's own colours key the whole model. They are ordinary rules keys — click one to change it in the Key panel.

An object's own keys win over the rules: in a hidden plant, a Show key on an object brings it in at its time — the way to build something up from nothing.

Show labels and measurements

While you animate, your own labels and measurements are put aside (they come back when you stop), so the animation shows only what it keys.

  1. Click the Labels row and move the time slider to when they should show.
  2. Add labels with the Label panel as usual — or press From the scene in the Key panel (ALT 1577) to bring in the ones you had — and set their colours there.
  3. Press Key labels (I, ALT 1575). From that time on they show; before the first key there are none.
  4. Later, pick the key (or key again) and untick a label in the Key panel to hide it from there on, or press None from here (ALT 1579).

The Measurements row works the same way with the measuring tools (ALT 1576, ALT 1578, ALT 1580). Labels and measurements stay where they are in the model — for a label that follows a moving part, add a callout from Overlay ▾.

Divide the animation into steps

  1. Click the Steps row, move the time slider to where a step begins and press Key (ALT 1513): a step from there to the next step (or 5 seconds).
  2. In the Key panel give it a Name and a Text — its card shows them while the step runs. Drag its edges on the Timeline to fit the keys it belongs to.
  3. Tick Pause at the end where the audience should get a moment; the play buttons and the play bar jump from step to step.

Explain something with a callout or a card

  1. Select the item in the 3D view and pick Callout on the selection in Overlay ▾ on the Timeline (ALT 942): a label with a leader line hangs from it for 3 seconds from the time slider. The same menu puts a title card, a caption or an info card on the screen.
  2. Click its bar to change its text, design, where it hangs and when it shows; drag the bar or its edges on the Timeline.
  3. To move it over time — to keep it clear of the item while the camera turns — press Key here (ALT 1338), move the time slider and drag the overlay in the 3D view: each drag adds a position key.

Build an animation with Claude Code

For an animation with hundreds of items, let Claude Code write it. You hand over two files: the brief from the Timeline's Export ▾ (how the animation file works — what to ask you, every option, patterns, your cards and a schema to check against) and an item list of the items you mean, made with its shortcuts.

  1. Export ▾ → Brief for AI… (ALT 1549) downloads animation-brief.md.
  2. To let Claude group by more than names and position — by system, area or discipline — first run a query in the SQL editor whose result has a fullname column and the attributes you care about.
  3. Select the items in the Hierarchy or the 3D view and press an Item list for AI shortcut: selected nodes (ALT 1550), children of selected (ALT 1551) or every leaf under the selection (ALT 1552). You get animation-items.csv: each item's name, fullname, parent, depth, how many items are under it, its model, the centre and size of its box where it stands now, and the SQL result's columns (at most 20 000 rows).
  4. To change an animation you already have, also Save it and give Claude the file.
  5. Tell Claude what you want, for example: "Build an animation from animation-brief.md and animation-items.csv: the whole plant hidden first, the skid built bottom-up, one step per level with a card, a label on each motor." The brief tells Claude to ask what it is missing — above all, what is visible at the start.
  6. Open the file Claude wrote with Open on the Animation ribbon (ALT 925). A file with a mistake is not opened; the message says what and where.

In a copy of the app's source, npm run anim:check animation.tdanim.json checks a file the way Open does (exit code 0 = it opens). Animation files from the earlier parts-and-clips version of the tool cannot be opened; make them again on the Timeline.

Present an animation

  1. Press Present on the Animation ribbon or the Timeline (ALT 1573): the 3D view alone with the play bar over it.
  2. Press play (or tap SPACE). At the end of a step that pauses, the animation waits with that step's card up; press play again (Continue, ALT 912) when your audience is ready.
  3. While it waits you can look around freely; Continue brings the camera back to the camera keys (unless you switched Camera keys off).
  4. Close on the play bar brings back the panels, ribbon and mode you had.

Save and share an animation

  1. Press Save (ALT 926). The animation is downloaded as a .tdanim.json file.
  2. Anyone with the same models loaded can open it with Open… (ALT 925) and play it.

Make a video

  1. Pick Export ▾ → Video on the Timeline (ALT 1410).
  2. Choose the Size (720p, 1080p or 1440p — the width follows the shape of the 3D view) and the Frame rate. With several scenes, pick All scenes (ALT 1664) — every scene, one after another — or One scene (ALT 1665) and which one under Scene (ALT 1666 steps through them).
  3. For one scene, What: the Whole scene (ALT 1667) or a Part (ALT 1668) — type its Start and End in seconds, or fill them from the In / out range you marked on the Timeline (ALT 1669) or the Step at the time slider (ALT 1670). An End past the scene's end holds its last picture. The line under the fields says how long the video will be.
  4. Quality: Draft (ALT 1413) renders each frame once and is quick; Best (ALT 1414) smooths every frame like a screenshot and takes several times longer. Wait at pausing steps holds the picture that many seconds (0 to 10, − / + ALT 1419 / ALT 1420) at the end of steps that pause there, with the step's card up. Untick Overlays (ALT 1418) to leave out the cards and callouts. ALT 1415 and ALT 1416 step through the sizes and frame rates, ALT 1417 switches between the whole scene and a part; Close (ALT 1662) leaves without exporting.
  5. Press Export (ALT 1411). The video is made frame by frame, so it plays smoothly however slow the computer is. Keep the tab in front while it runs: in a hidden tab the browser stops drawing, so the export waits — the tab's title says so — and carries on when you come back. Cancel (ALT 1412) stops it. The video is written to the browser's storage as it is made and then downloaded as .mp4, so a long one needs free disk space rather than memory.

The camera follows the camera keys; without any it stays where it is. The selection is cleared for the video; it comes back afterwards, with the scene that was open, the time slider, your own view and Camera keys as you had them.

Make step stills

For work instructions: Export ▾ → Step stills (PDF) (ALT 1494). Every step gets a page: its number and name, the step's last frame — everything in place, with its card and overlays — and its text under the picture.
